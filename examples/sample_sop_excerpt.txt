My interest in machine learning began during an undergraduate research project where I
built a model to detect anomalies in sensor data from a campus energy grid. That project
introduced me to the gap between models that perform well on benchmarks and models that
hold up under real-world distribution shift, a gap I have been chasing ever since.

Since then, I have worked on two research projects applying time-series models to
noisy, partially-labeled data, and I contributed to an open-source library for
data augmentation. I am drawn to research that treats robustness and generalization
not as an afterthought but as the central problem, and I want to continue that work
in a PhD program with faculty who study these questions rigorously.
